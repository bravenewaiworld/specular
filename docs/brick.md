# TrimUI Brick

Status: Implemented · Updated: 2026-10-19

The TrimUI Brick port (`devices/brick`): how Specular draws on the Brick's display, how each hardware service maps onto it, and what was measured there. The Brick (including the all-aluminium Hammer edition) is the reference device, so its measurements back many decisions in the other docs.

## Decisions

- **The TrimUI Brick is the reference device and the first port** (2026-10-12).
- **The stack was checked on the Brick before building on it** (2026-10-12). The spike's platform became `devices/brick/src`, and its measurements (`devices/brick/examples/s*`) stay as a regression check.
- **Our kernel implements `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC` on `/dev/fb0`** (2026-10-12), so games and the menus can be paced by the display (see [runtime.md](runtime.md)). Rejected: inferring the vsync phase from when a blocking swap returns (kept as the approach for a device whose kernel lacks the call).
- **Animations are paced by the vsync too: each frame is drawn only once the last one is on screen** (2026-10-18). An animation frame then goes on screen 16.6 ms after it starts, not 32 ms (see Findings), and a press during an animation is read while the loop waits, not after it was blocked in the swap. A thread waits for the vsync only after a frame an animation is to follow, so it stops when the animation does. Other frames (a game's, a one-off change) are drawn at once: a game's thread already paces its frames. Rejected: a thread ticking every refresh while Slint animates (one more thing to start and stop); waiting for the vsync on the UI thread (it holds up buttons and game frames for up to a refresh); `eglSwapInterval(0)` with our own waits (unmeasured on this driver, and it may flip at once and tear; with frames started at the vsync, interval 1 doesn't block anyway); a Slint timer at the refresh period (it drifts against the panel: frames doubled and dropped); holding every frame until the last is on screen (a one-off redraw between a vsync and a game's frame would put that and every later game frame a refresh late); game frames driving the UI while a game runs (a paused game sends none, and a game paced by a timer at its own rate too few).
- **Backlight levels 1–10 map to 16–255 on a curve** (2026-10-12), since perceived brightness is roughly logarithmic; level 1 stays readable indoors.
- **The LEDs are limited to about 40/255** (2026-10-12), as TrimUI's firmware does: they are very bright at full power.
- **Volume steps are 2 dB apart, the top one at 0 dB** (2026-10-13). Louder only clips.
- **Headphones are detected through the codec's jack input device, and the kernel switches the speaker** (2026-10-18). The jack only decides which level the volume keys and the gauge change. Rejected: the card's "Headphone Jack" control (the same state, but following it needs ALSA's control API and event subscription, where evdev is already read); switching the speaker from Specular (the driver already does it).
- **Each output's level goes to its own codec control, and the kernel puts the one in use into effect** (2026-10-19). Specular sets "Speaker Playback Volume" and "Headphone Playback Volume" whenever their level changes or the sound is muted, whichever output plays; the codec driver keeps both, so setting the other output's isn't heard, and switches between them with the jack. Rejected: Specular setting one shared control ("DAC volume") to the playing output's level on each jack change (it lagged the kernel's own switch, so the speaker's level played in the headphones for a moment, and while Specular wasn't running nothing switched it); keeping "DAC volume" as a fallback (Bricks update to a Crystaline OS with the two controls; on an older one each volume set fails and is logged as `hardware:`).
- **Rumble goes through padbridge's gamepad, like any game's** (2026-10-18). Rejected: the motor's own device, `regulator-haptic` (it would skip what padbridge does for every other program, and tie Specular to the Brick's motor, where a gamepad offering rumble is what any Linux handheld or controller has).
- **Long or detached runs on the device go over SSH, not adb** (2026-10-12). The stock adbd kills a session's processes when the session exits.

## Design

### Profile

1024×768 at 400 ppi (so 1 u is one pixel), refreshing at about 60.2 Hz. Buttons: the d-pad, A, B, X, Y, L1, R1, L2, R2, Select, Start, Menu, F1, F2, the volume keys, Power, and the FN switch, used as Mute. 14 RGB LEDs in chain order (LED-class names `sunxi_led0r` … `sunxi_led13b`): the 8 along the top show the first accent; F2 and F1 on the front, then the 4 under the shoulder buttons, show the second.

The OS's service manager runs Specular as the frontend and restarts it when it exits. If it fails 3 times within a minute, the system falls back to its `status` screen until the next boot (2026-10-13).

### Display platform

TrimUI's closed PowerVR driver has only PowerVR's "NULL window system": no windowing at all. A window surface created without a native window renders straight to the panel (`/dev/fb0`, whose buffers the display driver swaps on vsync). So there is no DRM, Wayland or X11, and Specular brings its own Slint platform: one full-screen window drawn with FemtoVG on a GLES 3 context, and its own event loop. The loop draws only when something changed or an animation runs; otherwise it sleeps in `poll()` until the next Slint timer, a wake-up from another thread, a button, or the next try to open a missing input device. All PowerVR specifics are in `egl.rs`.

Animations are paced by the display (`FramePacer`, in `specular-linux`): after presenting a frame that an animation is to follow, the vsync thread waits for the next `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC` (when that frame goes on screen) and wakes the loop, which draws nothing new until then. So each refresh gets one animation frame, started right after the vsync, and the swap returns at once. Any other frame, such as a game's new frame, is drawn as soon as it arrives, and its thread's own wait for the vsync paces it. While something animates over a running game (a cover zooming into it, a toast fading), the game's frames are drawn with the animation's next frame, a refresh later. With a kernel whose wait returns at once (TrimUI's), or without `/dev/fb0`, only the swap paces the loop: two refreshes from start to screen. A wait that fails counts as a vsync once it returns (our kernel's times out after 50 ms).

GL functions are looked up in `libGLESv2`'s exported symbols first, then through `eglGetProcAddress`. The EGL and GLES libraries are loaded at runtime.

Buttons come from two evdev devices: padbridge's virtual pad (`TRIMUI Player1`), which presents the Brick's buttons as a standard gamepad and reports the FN switch as `SW_TABLET_MODE`, and the power key of the AXP2202 power chip (`axp2202-pek`). Either can appear late or restart; they are reopened.

### Hardware services

| Service | How |
|---|---|
| Battery | power-supply class (`specular-linux`) |
| Backlight, panel on/off | Allwinner's `/dev/disp` ioctls (`DISP_LCD_SET_BRIGHTNESS`, `DISP_DEVICE_SWITCH`); the kernel has no `/sys/class/backlight` |
| LEDs | LED class (`specular-linux`), capped as above |
| Volume | the codec's "Speaker Playback Volume" and "Headphone Playback Volume" through `amixer` (`amixer -q -c audiocodec cset name='Speaker Playback Volume' 138`: one value, 0–255, 0.75 dB per step, 159 = 0 dB), each set to its own output's level. Bluetooth volume through bluez-alsa |
| Headphone jack | the input device `audiocodec sunxi Audio Jack` (`SW_HEADPHONE_INSERT`, `specular-linux`); the codec driver turns the speaker off while headphones are in, and plays the headphones' level |
| Rumble | force feedback (`FF_RUMBLE`) on padbridge's pad, `TRIMUI Player1`, the first gamepad offering it (`specular-linux`); padbridge passes it on to the motor |
| Sound | ALSA `default`; Bluetooth through bluez-alsa |
| Vsync | `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC` on `/dev/fb0`; without it, a timer paces games and the swap the menus |
| Sleep | `/usr/bin/sleepnow`, which also stops WiFi around sleep (its driver can't suspend while connected) |
| Power off, restart | `/sbin/poweroff`, `/sbin/reboot` |
| WiFi | wpa_supplicant on `wlan0`. Power on loads the XR829's `xr829_wlan` driver and starts the runit services `wpa_supplicant` and `wifi-dhcp`; power off stops them (waiting for wpa_supplicant to exit) and unloads the driver. On joining, the old address is flushed and udhcpc restarted, so it asks at once rather than at its next retry (up to 20 s away) |
| Bluetooth | BlueZ |
| Installer | `brick-install` (see [installer.md](installer.md)) |

Power-key presses are ignored from the request to sleep until 1.5 s after waking: the press that woke the device is delivered as it resumes, before the sleep command returns, and must not send it straight back to sleep.

### Fonts on TrimUI's stock system

TrimUI's stock system ships fontconfig but no fonts, and Slint's text layout (fontique) panics when fontconfig finds none. When fontconfig is present, `fonts.rs` writes Geist and a config naming it under `/tmp/specular-fonts` and points `FONTCONFIG_FILE` at it. Fontconfig is loaded at runtime, so our own system image can leave it out.

## Findings

Unless noted, measured with the CPU at the 600–816 MHz the `ondemand` governor picks for the load.

- (2026-10-12, device) TrimUI's GPU libraries offer OpenGL ES 3.2 and EGL 1.4, with only PowerVR's NULL window system. The display runs at about 60.2 Hz. Source: `devices/brick/examples/s0_toolchain.rs`, `s1_slint.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) A Rust `aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu` binary linked with the cross toolchain (GCC 6.4, glibc 2.23) runs, needing glibc 2.18 symbols at most. It is dynamic, since it loads the GPU libraries (and cores) at runtime. Source: `s0_toolchain.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) Slint's FemtoVG renderer on our EGL holds 60.2 fps, locked to vsync, with no late frames. A full redraw costs about 4 ms of CPU; the rest of each 16.6 ms is waiting for vsync. An idle screen draws nothing: about 0.5 % CPU. Source: `s1_slint.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) `eglGetProcAddress` also returns core GL functions in our own context (EGL 1.4 doesn't promise it). Source: `s1_slint.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) A press reaches the screen in 7–10 ms on average (worst 12 ms), from the kernel's timestamp to the end of the present, with evdev read inside our event loop and injected presses (padbridge's own hop not included). Source: `s1_slint.rs`, `inject.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) An emulator thread with a shared, surfaceless GL context can upload CPU-made frames into textures and hand them over with a GL fence; Slint shows them as borrowed textures, and a texture goes back only after the present that replaced it. No tearing: each read-back frame held exactly one emulator frame. Source: `s4_frames.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) Drawing each game frame through the Slint scene costs 2.8–3.3 ms of UI-thread CPU per frame, against 2.1–2.3 ms for our own one-quad blit. With the emulator paced by the display, latency is the same either way: a press reaches the end of the present 24–25 ms later on average (33 ms worst). An emulator on its own clock (60.0988 Hz) drifts against the display (60.2 Hz), and its frames sometimes wait a whole refresh: 21–38 ms on average. Source: `s4_frames.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) Draw-then-swap puts a frame on screen 32 ms (two vsyncs) after it was started: the swap waits for the previous frame's flip, and the new frame flips at the vsync after that. Starting each frame only once the previous one is on screen takes 16 ms, at the same 60 fps. Also delaying the start by 10 ms takes 6 ms, still at 60 fps; at 13 ms, frames start to miss the vsync. Measured with the display driver's record of the flip (`/dev/fb0`'s pan offset), which may differ from the panel by up to a vsync. Source: `s5_scanout.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, upstream) TrimUI's display driver has a vsync wait internally (`fb_wait_for_vsync`), but its `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC` ioctl is stubbed out and returns at once. Source: the Brick's kernel source.
- (2026-10-12, device) With our kernel's `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC`, the wait returns when the display takes a new frame, and buffer switches follow it by a median 0.1 ms. Waiting for it, then drawing: the swap no longer blocks (about 1 ms), 60 fps, 16.6 ms from frame start to on screen; with a 10 ms frame delay, 6.5 ms. Source: `s5_scanout.rs`.
- (2026-10-18, upstream) Our kernel's (Crystaline OS's) `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC` returns at once while the display is off, and fails with `ETIMEDOUT` after 50 ms without a vsync. Since 2026-10-13 it doesn't take the framebuffer lock, which a page flip holds until its vsync; the menus' vsync thread waits right after a present, so it relies on this (with the lock, each wait would end a refresh late: 30 fps). Source: Crystaline OS's `drivers/video/fbdev/sunxi/disp2/disp/dev_fb.c`.
- (2026-10-12, device) The spike used 20 MB resident with the scene, fonts and frame textures. The first frame was on screen about 1.1 s after `main()` started. Source: the spike (`devices/brick/examples`).
- (2026-10-12, device) Geist from 27 to 96 px is clean on the panel. Source: `s1_slint.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) The GL context and textures survive sleep, and rendering resumes without errors. Source: `s1_slint.rs`.
- (2026-10-12, device) After `sleepnow` restarts WiFi, the WiFi driver can leave the system in a state where every swap waits about two vsyncs (25–50 fps instead of 60), with "miss interrupt" warnings from the XR829. Restarting WiFi again fixes it. Tracked in Crystaline OS's roadmap. Source: Brick sleep tests.
- (2026-10-12, inferred) Press to photons: a press waits about 8 ms on average for the next frame to read it, the frame then takes 32 ms (draw-then-swap) or about 6 ms (paced, 10 ms frame delay) to go on screen, and scanout takes up to 16 ms to reach a given row: about 48 ms or about 22 ms to the middle of the screen. Source: the measurements above.
- (2026-10-13, device) TrimUI's ALSA set-up has a software volume ("Soft Volume Master"), but the driver refuses writes to it. Source: `amixer` on the Brick.
- (2026-10-14, device) The XR829 carries WiFi and Bluetooth on one chip; loading and unloading the WiFi driver powers only the WiFi part. Source: `devices/brick/src/wifi.rs`.
- (2026-10-18, upstream) Our kernel's codec machine driver reports the jack through ASoC (`snd_soc_card_jack_new("sunxi Audio Jack")`, `snd_soc_jack_report`). That makes an input device named `audiocodec sunxi Audio Jack` (card short name, then jack name) with `SW_HEADPHONE_INSERT` and `SW_MICROPHONE_INSERT`, and the card controls "Headphone Jack" and "HpSpeaker Jack". The report also switches the DAPM pins: "Headphone" on and "HpSpeaker" off while headphones are in, and powering "HpSpeaker" down drops the speaker amplifier's enable GPIO (PH6). Source: Crystaline OS's `linux/sound/soc/sunxi/sun50iw10-sndcodec.c` (`sunxi_jack_pins`, `sunxi_card_init`), `sun50iw10-codec.c` (`sunxi_codec_hpspeaker_event`), `sound/core/jack.c` (`snd_jack_dev_register`), `sound/soc/soc-jack.c`.
- (2026-10-18, upstream) The jack state arrives about 110 ms after plugging in (a 10 ms delayed work, then 100 ms before the jack type is read) and 10 ms after unplugging. The switch, the speaker and the input event change in the same report. Source: `sun50iw10-sndcodec.c` (`jack_interrupt`, `sunxi_check_hs_detect_status`).
- (2026-10-19, upstream) Crystaline OS's codec driver has a volume control for each output, "Speaker Playback Volume" and "Headphone Playback Volume": one value each, 0–255 at 0.75 dB per step, 159 = 0 dB, up to +72 dB (which only clips). It keeps both, and puts the level of the output in use into the codec's one volume register as that output powers up (as playback starts, or as the jack switches outputs during playback; the speaker's before its amplifier is on), and when the control of the output in use changes. So neither level plays on the other output, even for a moment after plugging in. A jack change while nothing plays takes effect when playback next starts. Bluetooth audio doesn't go through the codec. TrimUI's stock kernel has neither control, so Specular can't set the volume there. Source: Crystaline OS's `docs/audio.md`.
- (2026-10-18, inferred) Headphones already in at boot are only known if the codec raises its jack-in interrupt when detection is switched on; otherwise the kernel, and so Specular, take the jack as empty until it is next plugged in. After resume the driver reports "empty" unless that interrupt comes within 10 ms (`sunxi_hs_init_work` with `RESUME_IRQ`). Specular follows whatever the kernel reports, so the volume keys always change the level that plays. Source: `sun50iw10-sndcodec.c` (`sunxi_card_dev_probe`, `sunxi_card_resume`).
- (2026-10-18, upstream) padbridge's pad offers `FF_RUMBLE` (16 effects) and passes each upload, erase, play and stop on to the motor's device, `regulator-haptic`, which it opens on the first request. Since 2026-10-18 the pad always offers rumble, even with the motor missing when padbridge starts (uploads then fail with `EIO` until it is found; it is looked for once a second); before, it offered rumble only if the motor was there at start. An upload waits until padbridge answers, with no time limit. Source: Crystaline OS's `userspace/padbridge/padbridge.c` (`handle_rumble`, `motor`), `docs/leds-and-rumble.md`.
- (2026-10-18, upstream) The motor's strength is its supply voltage: 2.0 V + 1.5 V × magnitude / 65536, from the strong motor's magnitude, or the weak one's if the strong is 0; magnitude 0 turns it off. So any strength above 0 runs it at 2.0 V or more. Source: Crystaline OS's `docs/leds-and-rumble.md`.
- (2026-10-18, upstream) Each LED brightness write sends a frame down the chain, and Specular writes 42 (3 per LED) for each change of the LEDs; a frame is followed by a 300 µs gap. Source: Crystaline OS's `docs/leds-and-rumble.md`.
- (2026-10-18, inferred) Specular's rumble effect is erased by the kernel when Specular's file on the pad closes, however Specular ends, which stops it (an erase plays the effect at 0 first), and padbridge passes the erase on to the motor. The EV_FF events Specular writes to the pad also reach its readers, including Specular's own button reader, which ignores them. Source: Linux `drivers/input/ff-core.c` (`erase_effect`), `drivers/input/input.c` (`input_get_disposition`).
- (2026-10-19, device) Crystaline OS's padbridge deadlocks on a game's first rumble effect: to find the motor it opens every `/dev/input/event*` in `readdir` order, which lists the newest first, so its own pad comes before the motor. Specular's effect upload (`EVIOCSFF`) waits for padbridge's answer while holding the pad's lock, so padbridge's open of the pad never returns. The pad's buttons stop, and killing padbridge then leaves the uploading thread spinning in the kernel (`uinput_request_done`, RCU stalls) until a restart. With a padbridge patched to find the motor by its sysfs name (`/sys/class/input/eventN/device/name`), Specular's rumble works: following Contra's sound, the motor's regulator (`axp2202-bldo3`) switched on and off 82 times in 30 s at 2.0–2.8 V. Source: `/proc/<pid>/stack` of both, padbridge's `open_input`, the regulator's sysfs state.
- (2026-10-19, device) Specular's rumble recovers from padbridge restarting: `rumble: TRIMUI Player1 is gone`, one `No such device`, then `working again` on the new pad. Source: the log.
- (2026-10-19, device) Menu animations are paced by the vsync: each animation's frames have a median of 16.6 ms between them. Short ones (screens opening and closing, a focus move) have 0–2 late frames each, the longest 26–34 ms, often the first; a 6 s held scroll had 342–355 frames with 1–13 late (one 83 ms hitch). The UI thread's CPU a frame is 4–6.6 ms, so the late frames aren't its drawing. The CPU ran at the 1.0–1.4 GHz `ondemand` picked. Animations as a game starts or ends (the picture zooming into and out of it) run at half rate: medians of 28.6–35.0 ms, most frames late. Source: frame statistics' `ui:` lines.
- (2026-10-19, device) An idle screen presents nothing and doesn't wait for the vsync: over 10 s, Specular made no `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC` call and no buffer swap, and used 0.2 % of a core with WiFi off. Its main loop woke once a second only for the frame statistics' timer (a developer setting). Separately: with WiFi on Auto, WiFi scans for about the first 90 s after start-up, while the art downloader waits for a network. Source: `strace -f -p` of Specular.
- (2026-10-19, device) At start-up with Speaker 12 and Headphones 10, the controls read 138 and 132, as the step formula says; muted, both read 0. This OS's codec has no "DAC volume" control (older ones had only that; see DEVELOPMENT.md). Source: `amixer -c audiocodec cget`.
- (2026-10-19, device) A switch event written to padbridge's pad (to fake the FN switch) lasts only until padbridge next reports its state, which writing button events to the pad also makes it do; and a d-pad hold written there ends early. So scripted holds go through `SPECULAR_SCRIPT`, not `inject`. Source: Specular's `sound:` lines, `inject` holds.
- (2026-10-19, device) In Crystaline OS's image of 2026-10-19, `/usr/bin/dufs`'s `security.capability` attribute is one byte (`\x01`) instead of the 20 bytes the image build writes (`CAP_NET_BIND_SERVICE`), so the kernel ignores it, and dufs, run as www-data, can't listen on port 80 (`Permission denied`): file transfer can't start. Source: BusyBox `getfattr` on the device, `tools/system/tar-with-owners.py` in Crystaline OS.
- (2026-10-19, device) With "Sleep after" at 30 s, the device stayed awake for over 2 minutes idle while a PC was connected over USB (`power: a PC is connected over USB (usb0, 192.168.7.2)`). Source: the log, `uptime`.

## Open questions

- Does rumble feel right once padbridge is fixed (see Findings)? The motor running in a game with rumble (Drill Dozer, Pokémon Pinball, WarioWare: Twisted!, or a PS1 game: Metal Gear Solid has no rumble in its first 90 s, so play further); the motor stopping when the menu opens, on sleep and on quitting; the "Rumble" strength changing it; and, with "Follow the sound" on, short rumbles on explosions and drums but not steady music. With frame statistics on, the line over the game shows the rumble's strength. Also: how often mGBA's per-frame strengths make Specular upload (each upload sets the motor's voltage over I2C).
- Do the lights' effects look right on the LEDs: the low-battery pulse (three half-second pulses as the battery reaches 15 %), and the flash on saving (0.3 s white)? `effects:` lines in the log say when each starts.
- Why do the animations as a game starts or ends run at half rate (see Findings), and what makes an animation's first frame late? Does a game keep its 60.2 fps and latency with a toast or the volume gauge animating over it? `s5_scanout vsync 0` measures the pacing without Slint.
- Do the jack and the two volume controls work as the OS describes them (built 2026-10-19)? Each output at its own level, the other's changes unheard, no moment at the speaker's level after plugging in, Bluetooth volume unaffected by the codec controls. DEVELOPMENT.md has the check.
- Are headphones already in at boot, or through sleep, reported (see Findings)? Start up and wake with them in, and check the log and which level plays.
